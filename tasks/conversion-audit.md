# Conversion audit — storefront + business model

> Audited 2026-08-09 against `main` @ `dce9876`, plus the live site at
> meltingmomentscakes.com. Scope: what would make more visitors order, given a
> hard constraint that anything proposed must run **without recurring manual
> effort** and at **near-zero marginal cost**.

---

## The finding that matters most

Every "autonomous" idea below needs the same thing: something that wakes up on a
schedule and acts. **That already exists and is proven in production.**

`apps/api/app/services/batch_scheduler.py` runs a loop inside the app's own
lifespan, ticking once a minute, guarded by a Postgres advisory lock so exactly
one uvicorn worker sweeps, plus `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` on the rows it claims.
Its own docstring is explicit: *"There is no queue in this stack — no Celery, no
cron, nothing that survives the container."*

So the marginal infrastructure cost of every lifecycle campaign in this document
is **zero**. No Celery, no Redis queue, no third-party marketing platform, no new
container, no new secret. A second sweep function on the same tick, with its own
advisory-lock key, is the entire delivery mechanism.

Email delivery is likewise already built: Resend, with `EmailLog` recording
template, recipient, status, `resend_id` and error per send. The only running
cost is Resend's per-email price.

**This is the leverage.** The machinery is done. What is missing is that nothing
currently uses it for anything except transactional mail.

## Audit: what's already strong

Worth stating plainly, because it changes what's worth building:

- **Guest checkout is real.** `ensureCheckoutAuth` mints a guest rather than
  forcing registration. The single biggest classic checkout killer is absent.
- **Cart upsell exists** — "You may also like" renders on the cart page.
- **New-customer coupon tray** applies a first-order code in one tap, and refuses
  to render unless `/promo-codes/featured` proves a campaign is live. Correct.
- **Analytics coverage is genuinely good** — ~40 events including
  `add_to_cart_failed`, `search_no_results`, `product_unavailable`,
  `checkout_error`, `free_delivery_unlocked`, `promo_failed`. You can *measure*
  every recommendation below before and after.
- **Delivery promise is the differentiator and it's front and centre** — "Get it
  in 60 minutes" on every tile and PDP, live zone detection, free-delivery
  banner keyed to the visitor's actual area.
- **The abandoned basket is already partially instrumented.** `Cart` stores the
  live courier quote — fee, cost, zone, distance, coordinates, and the *error* if
  a quote failed — with a comment noting a basket that was abandoned is as
  informative as one that converted.

The site is not broken. These are gaps in the *revenue layer*, not the product.

## Tier 1 — highest return, fully autonomous, near-zero cost

### 1. Abandoned-cart recovery

**Gap.** Nothing. No email fires when a basket is left. This is the single
largest recoverable revenue line on the site.

**Why it's the top item here.** For food delivery, abandonment is usually a
timing problem, not a rejection — someone browsed at 3pm and got interrupted. A
reminder at 6pm catches the same craving. Recovery rates in this category are
routinely 5–15% of abandoned baskets.

**What exists.** `Cart` with items, totals, timestamps, and the live delivery
quote. `EmailLog` for dedupe. The scheduler.

**What's missing.** A guest cart has no email address on it. Email is collected
at checkout but never written back to the cart, so a basket abandoned *before*
the checkout form is unreachable, and one abandoned *during* checkout is
reachable but nothing stores the link.

**Build.** Persist email to `carts` at the first checkout step; sweep for carts
idle >1h with items and an email and no prior send; two touches (1h, 24h); stop
on order or on cart clear. Dedupe on `EmailLog`.

**Cost.** Resend per-email only. **Manual effort after launch: none.**

---

### 2. Product reviews, solicited automatically on delivery

**Gap.** No review model anywhere in the codebase. Grep for "review" across
`apps/api/app/models` and `apps/web/components` returns exactly one file — the
Cloudflare Turnstile component.

**Why it converts.** The live PDP for a 70 AED product is: title, one sentence,
price, "Get it in 60 minutes", ADD TO CART. There is no social proof at any point
in the funnel. For food bought sight-unseen, other people's verdict *is* the
product photography's other half. This is the highest-leverage single addition to
the PDP.

**Why it's autonomous.** This is the key property. Reviews are the rare asset
that, once the solicitation is wired to fire on the `delivered` status event, is
generated by customers forever with zero ongoing effort. Content that writes
itself.

**What exists.** `order_status_events` — added in the most recent work, one row
per status transition, written by a SQLAlchemy listener on `Order.status` so no
writer can forget it. A sweep for orders that reached `delivered` N days ago is
a single query against a table that is guaranteed complete.

**Build.** `product_reviews` (order-item-scoped, so every review is provably from
a real purchase — no fake-review surface); a tokenised one-click star rating in
the email; rating + count on PDP and product tiles; admin moderation queue.

**Note.** This is on `ROADMAP.md` under P3 Feature Requests, still unchecked. It
is badly mis-tiered — it belongs at the top.

---

### 3. Free-delivery progress in the *cart*, not just checkout

**Gap.** `free_delivery_threshold` is modelled per delivery polygon, surfaced on
the home banner, and surfaced at checkout (`checkout/page.tsx:520–532`,
`1161–1168`). The cart page mentions it **zero times** — grep returns 0.

**Why it matters.** By checkout the basket is already decided. The cart is where
someone is still willing to add a second box. Telling them "18 AED more for free
delivery" at checkout mostly just informs them they're paying delivery.

**Build.** Move the existing threshold calculation into the cart page and a
sticky mobile bar. `analytics.freeDeliveryUnlocked` is already defined, so it's
measurable on day one.

**Cost.** Front-end only. No new endpoint, no new model, no running cost, and it
never needs touching again.

---

### 4. Reorder prompts on the customer's own rhythm

**Gap.** No post-purchase email beyond delivery confirmation. A customer who
ordered brownies six weeks ago and enjoyed them hears nothing, ever.

**Why it's the best margin on the list.** Repeat purchase costs no acquisition
spend. For a dessert brand with a 40–70 AED basket, the profitable customer is
the fourth order, not the first.

**Build.** Sweep for a customer's last delivered order; fire a one-tap
"order it again" that pre-fills the basket from `order_items`. Derive cadence
from that customer's own median inter-order gap rather than a fixed 30 days —
the data is already in `orders`.

**Cost.** Resend only. Fires forever without supervision.

## Tier 2 — high value, autonomous once built

### 5. Occasion capture → self-firing annual reminders

The homepage sells this explicitly: *"Birthdays, Eid, weddings, office orders.
Boxes for the days that matter."* Nothing captures a date.

One optional field at checkout — *who is this for, and when is their birthday* —
builds a permanent calendar that fires itself every year with no input. Eid and
Ramadan are fixed, shared, and already named in `CaterSection`. For a UAE
celebration bakery this is likely the highest-margin autonomous channel that
exists, because the reminder arrives when intent is at its annual peak and the
recipient has already tasted the product.

### 6. Back-in-stock / "baked today" alerts

`analytics.productUnavailable` already fires with `reason: 'out_of_stock'` — so
**you can size this opportunity from existing Umami data before writing any
code.** A one-tap "tell me when it's back" converts a dead PDP from a bounce into
a captured, high-intent lead, and the alert fires off the inventory change.

### 7. WhatsApp notifications

On `ROADMAP.md`, unchecked, and correctly flagged as "very popular in UAE" —
open rates far exceed email here. **Caveat against your brief:** per-message
pricing is real, and Business API template approval is genuine one-time manual
setup. It is autonomous *after* launch, not before. Do it after Tier 1.

## Business-model findings

### A. The highest-value product has no self-serve funnel

`CustomOrder` is fully built — due date, capacity slots, lead days, deposits,
reference photos, flavour, size, servings, cake message, branch assignment,
status lifecycle. `BusinessSettings` carries `custom_orders_per_day`,
`custom_order_lead_days`, `custom_order_max_days_ahead`.

**There is no storefront route for it.** `apps/web/app/[locale]` has no custom,
cater, or booking page. The "PLAN AN ORDER" CTA in `CaterSection` falls back to
`/contact` (`CaterSection.tsx:59`).

And the model's own comment says the quiet part: an Instagram order has no user
and no order, *"and the person taking it on their phone has a name and a number
and nothing else"* — noting DMs are **most of them**.

So the highest-AOV, highest-margin product — wedding, corporate and event cakes —
is sold entirely by hand, in DMs, at the cost of someone's attention per order,
and the storefront's only offer is a contact form. Every one of those DMs is
manual effort that a booking funnel deletes permanently. The capacity check, the
lead-time rule and the deposit already exist server-side; what's missing is the
page.

**This is the largest single conversion and effort win available**, and it is
squarely on-brief: build once, no manual effort thereafter.

### B. A promise the checkout can't keep

The USP marquee on the live homepage advertises **"GIFT BOXES WITH A HANDWRITTEN
NOTE"**. Grep for `gift_message` / `gift_wrap` across `apps/api/app` and
`apps/web` returns nothing. There is a generic `Order.notes` field, but no gift
message anywhere in checkout.

A customer who came for the gifting proposition reaches checkout, finds no way to
say who it's from, and either abandons or orders something impersonal. Gifting
also raises AOV and acquires the *recipient* as a future customer for free.

### C. Loyalty and gift cards — reconsider narrowly

`apps/api/app/models/marketing.py` documents that gift cards, loyalty and house
accounts were built and removed, because none was ever wired into a selling path:
*"no order ever earned a point, no payment ever drew down a balance."* Deleting a
money-shaped hole was the right call.

But a **gift card** is the one autonomous acquisition tool where the buyer
recruits the recipient for you, at no marketing cost. Worth revisiting as a
narrow, fully-wired feature — never as the ledger that was removed.

## Prerequisite — do this before any marketing email

**There is no marketing consent flag and no unsubscribe mechanism anywhere.**
Grep for `marketing_consent`, `opt_in`, `unsubscribe`, `newsletter` across the
API and both front-ends returns nothing. `User` has `phone_verified_at` but no
marketing preference.

Every campaign in Tier 1 and 2 is a non-transactional send. Shipping any of them
without a consent field, a one-click unsubscribe honoured at send time, and a
suppression check in `email_service` risks the sending domain's reputation — and
a burnt domain takes the *transactional* order confirmations down with it.

This is small (one column, one endpoint, one guard in `_send`) and it is not
optional. It gates items 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6.

## Storefront polish found while auditing

Low effort, no running cost:

- **PDP is too thin for food.** No allergens, no ingredients, no weight or
  serving guidance beyond the title. For a category where people ask "is there
  nut in this" before buying, this loses orders silently.
- **The recently-viewed slot downsells.** On the *500g* Nutella Cookie Melt PDP,
  the only module below the fold offers the *250g* at 40 AED. The highest-intent
  position on the page is arguing the customer down 30 AED. It should cross-sell
  a different flavour or a mix box.
- **"1 TREATS"** on the Eggless and Extras category tiles — unpluralised count.
- **Category slugs are `cat-cookiemelt`, `cat-cookies`.** Non-semantic prefixes
  weaken both search ranking and AI-answer citation for exactly the queries this
  business wants to win ("cookie delivery Sharjah").
